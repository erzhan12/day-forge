Day Forge is a personal daily-schedule assistant. You plan a day as a list of time blocks, optionally pull in tasks and calendar events from services you connect, and optionally ask an AI assistant to draft or adjust the day. This page describes exactly what that stores, what leaves the server, and how to get rid of it.

1. Data you give us

Account

  • Your username and a salted hash of your password (the password itself is never stored in a readable form).
  • A session cookie and a CSRF cookie, used to keep you signed in and to protect form submissions. There are no advertising, tracking, or third-party analytics cookies.

Schedule content

  • Your schedules and their time blocks: title, start and end time, category, completion state, and ordering.
  • Your categories, day templates, and planning rules.
  • Your settings: time zone, day-window start and end, and interface preferences such as the selected theme.
  • Daily reviews, including any notes you write and the computed completion ratio for the day.

AI interactions

When you use the AI assistant, an audit row is written for each request. It records your most recent message verbatim, a SHA-256 fingerprint of the conversation it belonged to, the model's response, and the outcome. Because your message is stored verbatim, do not type passwords, API keys, or other secrets into the assistant.

Credentials for services you connect

Every integration is optional and off until you connect it. When you do connect one, the credential is encrypted at rest with a symmetric key held in the server's environment, and is decrypted only at the moment a request to that provider is made:

  • Apple Calendar / CalDAV — the server URL, your username, and an app-specific password.
  • Todoist — your personal API token.
  • Habitica — your Habitica user ID and API token.
  • Google Calendar — your Google account identifier and email address, plus OAuth refresh and access tokens.

2. Google Calendar and Google user data

If you connect Google Calendar, Day Forge requests these scopes and no others:

  • calendar.readonly — list your calendars and read events. This scope cannot create, change, or delete anything in your Google Calendar, and Day Forge never attempts to.
  • openid and userinfo.email — identify which Google account was connected, so multiple accounts can be told apart and disconnected individually.

Events are fetched on demand for the day you are viewing and held in a short-lived server-side cache (five minutes by default) so that repeated views do not re-query Google. Event titles and times are shown to you in the calendar panel and are used only for display and for the optional "add this event to my schedule" action. Google Calendar data is never included in any prompt sent to an AI provider.

Day Forge's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

You can disconnect a Google account at any time from the Settings page, which deletes the stored tokens, or revoke access directly at myaccount.google.com/permissions.

3. What leaves the server

The AI provider

The AI features send data to a third-party large-language-model provider over an OpenAI-compatible API. The operator of this deployment chooses that provider, so the exact company depends on how this instance is configured. Two features send data there:

  • Draft generation sends your day template, your planning rules, and a summary of recent days — block titles, times, categories, and completion ratios — so the model can propose a schedule.
  • Chat sends the current day's blocks together with the entire visible conversation — not just your latest message — on every turn. The conversation lives only in your browser tab; clearing the thread, reloading the page, or moving to a different date resets it. If you have discussed something you would rather not send again, clear the thread before your next message.

Task text from Todoist and Habitica, and event titles from any calendar, are never included in AI prompts. They are display-only.

Services you connected

When a task or calendar panel is open, the server makes requests to iCloud, Todoist, Habitica, or Google on your behalf using the credentials you supplied. Marking a Todoist or Habitica task complete from Day Forge sends that completion to the provider. Each of those providers has its own privacy policy governing what it does with the request.

Nothing else

Day Forge does not sell or rent your data, does not share it for advertising, and runs no third-party analytics or tracking scripts. Data may be disclosed only if required by law.

4. Retention and deletion

  • Schedules, reviews, templates, rules, and AI audit rows are kept until they are deleted.
  • Disconnecting an integration in Settings deletes the stored credential or token for it immediately.
  • Deleting a time block or schedule in the app deletes it from the database.
  • To have your whole account and its data removed, use the contact route below. Backups of the underlying database, where the operator keeps them, may retain copies for a short period after deletion.

5. Security

  • All traffic is served over HTTPS.
  • Third-party credentials and OAuth tokens are encrypted at rest with Fernet (AES-128-CBC with an HMAC), keyed from the server environment rather than from the database.
  • Passwords are stored using Django's password hashing.
  • No system is perfectly secure, and no absolute guarantee of security can be given.

6. Children

Day Forge is not directed at children and is not intended for use by anyone under 16.

7. Changes to this policy

This policy may be updated as the application changes. The date at the top of the page reflects the most recent revision. Continuing to use Day Forge after a change means you accept the revised policy.
